Generator tuning

Runtime knobs for the simulation — arrivals, time pacing, session length, and completion/rating behavior. All take effect on the next tick.

Tick interval (s)
Wall-clock seconds between generator ticks — how often the loop wakes up and does anything at all. Distinct from "sim. seconds / tick" below. Fixed at {{ tick_seconds }}s, not editable here any more: every tick now also publishes a Kafka event for each in-progress item, and watch-summary-service's inactivity-gap detection depends on that cadence staying put — see client-input/README.md.
Sim. seconds / tick
How much simulated watch-time each tick advances an in-progress item. Together with an item's duration, this determines how many ticks it takes to finish or hit the abandon point.
Low (5): a 90-min movie (5,400s) needs ~1,080 ticks to finish — sessions sit "in progress" for a long time even with a fast tick interval.
High (3,600): a whole movie can finish in a single tick — items barely show up as "in progress" before they're done.
Arrival probability
The chance arrivals happen at all this tick — a single per-tick coin flip, not applied per-user. Controls traffic volume — how often people show up — and has nothing to do with what happens once someone's watching.
Low (0.05): new users trickle in rarely; active-session count grows slowly.
High (1.0): a batch of new users shows up on every single tick, until the whole user pool is active.
New active users / tick
When arrivals do happen, the max batch size (actual count is random between 1 and this) — every user in that batch starts watching immediately, there's no separate "generated but idle" step.
Low (0): stops automatic arrivals entirely, regardless of arrival probability — use "Open sessions now" to add users manually instead.
High (50): up to 50 users can go active in one tick — can exhaust a small user pool in a couple of ticks.
Max items / session
Ceiling for a per-session cap rolled once when a user becomes active (uniformly random between 1 and this value) — how many items one active stretch watches before the user goes inactive again.
Low (1): every session watches exactly one item then closes — short-lived sessions, closer to a single-watch model.
High (100): sessions can binge up to 100 items before closing — active-session count stays elevated since sessions rarely free up.
Abandon probability
Chance any given item is abandoned partway through rather than watched to completion — rolled once per item, independent of arrivals/traffic. This is what actually drives the finish/abandon split in the tables below.
Low (0.05): almost everything finishes — the "abandon" badge rarely appears.
High (0.9): almost every watch gets cut short — most rows show "abandon" with watched_seconds well under the item's duration.
Rating probability
Chance a finisher rates the item after it ends. Users who abandoned rate at a fixed fraction of this (they rate less often) — there's no separate dial for that.
Low (0.1): ratings become rare — de.iu.Rating.V002 gets sparse traffic relative to Watch.Event.
High (1.0): virtually every finisher rates — Rating events roughly track ItemFinishedEvents 1:1 for finishers.
